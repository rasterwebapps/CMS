# Business Requirements Document — Approvals, Budget & Gate Pass

## 1. Executive Summary / Business Objective
`CORE_REQUIREMENTS_AND_GAP_ANALYSIS.md` identified the source SRS's Workflow & Approvals (§3.13), Gate Pass (§3.15), and Consignment Stock (§3.16) sections as "genuinely reusable across any vertical" or "generalizing fine once decoupled from pharma framing," while flagging Budget & Finance (§3.7, GAP-27) as fiscal-year-locked and CRM & Ticketing (§3.8) as needing a generic, non-hospital-specific shape. The business objective of this sub-module is a single financial-control and physical-movement-control layer any deployment can run on top of its Purchase Requisitions/Orders and physical stock: budget visibility (not hard enforcement, by deliberate choice — see Business Rules), a genuinely generic multi-level/parallel approval-routing engine usable across document types, controlled outward/inward movement of items via Gate Pass, a clean side-ledger for goods a supplier owns until consumed, and a simple internal service/complaint ticket — none of it branded toward any one industry.

## 2. Stakeholders
- Location/finance staff (allocate and monitor Budgets)
- Workflow authors (design Approval Workflows — typically admin-tier)
- Document owners/approvers (act on their stage of an Approval Instance)
- Bypass-authority holders (Exception handling)
- Gate Pass requesters, approvers, and security/gate-verifiers (three distinct roles)
- Consignment-receiving store staff and finance/procurement staff (record consumption)
- Service Ticket requesters, coordinators (assign), technicians (resolve), and closers

## 3. Business Rules
| ID | Rule | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| BR-AG-1 | A Budget's period is a plain date range, not tied to this app's Academic Year/term calendar. | Keeps the module industry-agnostic — a hospital deployment has no academic year at all (Decision Log, 2026-09-08 "Budget allocation slice"). |
| BR-AG-2 | Budget consumption is computed live from sent (non-`PENDING`) Purchase Order line totals only — Purchase Requisition spend is never counted, since a requisition carries no monetary value. | Only `PurchaseOrder` line totals represent real committed spend (Decision Log, 2026-09-08 "Budget allocation slice"). |
| BR-AG-3 | Budget over-allocation is purely informational — no submit path on Purchase Requisition/Order is blocked by it. Real hard enforcement is a deliberate future escalation, not built here. | Explicit scope instruction in the original plan (Decision Log, 2026-09-08 "Budget allocation slice"). |
| BR-AG-4 | An Approval Workflow step's "who may act" is a real reference to the platform's existing `Permission` table, never a hardcoded role or a new parallel "approver" concept. | Reuses the exact primitive every other `@PreAuthorize` check in the app already uses, rather than introducing a second authorization concept (Decision Log, 2026-09-08 "Multi-level approval routing slice"). |
| BR-AG-5 | Sequential vs. parallel routing is represented by one `stepOrder` integer: shared value = parallel (all must approve before the stage clears), distinct values = sequential. | Simplest correct representation of both without two different code paths (Decision Log, 2026-09-08 "Multi-level approval routing slice"). |
| BR-AG-6 | A workflow's `minAmount` threshold applies only to `PURCHASE_ORDER` workflows — a `PURCHASE_REQUISITION` workflow always applies unconditionally when active for its location. | Purchase Requisition carries no monetary value, same finding as BR-AG-2 (Decision Log, 2026-09-08 "Multi-level approval routing slice"). |
| BR-AG-7 | Starting an Approval Instance is a deliberate, separate user action — submitting/sending the underlying document does not automatically trigger or block on it. | Rewiring two already-shipped, heavily-used state machines to hard-block on a brand-new engine deserves its own reviewed slice with real usage first, not a same-session addition (Decision Log, 2026-09-08 "Multi-level approval routing slice"). |
| BR-AG-8 | A single rejection at any approval stage immediately fails the whole instance; there is no partial-rejection/re-route/resubmit flow. | Standard approval-chain semantics; a recovery flow is separately-scoped design work not implied by the base requirement (Decision Log, 2026-09-08 "Multi-level approval routing slice"). |
| BR-AG-9 | A bypass requires its own dedicated `INVENTORY_APPROVAL_BYPASS` permission, never the step's own required permission, and always carries a required structured reason (fixed enum) plus free-text notes. | "Approve normally" and "override as an exception" are genuinely different operations under the operation-wise permission mapping rule (Decision Log, 2026-09-08 "Exception handling slice"). |
| BR-AG-10 | Gate Pass approval and gate (security) verification are always two separate steps/permissions, even when the same person holds both. | Matches the ER doc's own `ApprovedBy`/`SecurityVerifiedBy` split and the operation-wise permission mapping rule — approving a request and physically checking an item at the gate are different operations (Decision Log, 2026-09-08 "Gate Pass slice"). |
| BR-AG-11 | A non-returnable Gate Pass closes as soon as it is gate-verified; a returnable one becomes "in effect" (`GATE_VERIFIED`) and can go overdue, derived at read time from `expectedReturnDate`, never stored. | Mirrors the already-shipped `LoanableItemIssue`'s overdue posture exactly (Decision Log, 2026-09-08 "Gate Pass slice"). |
| BR-AG-12 | Receiving Consignment stock posts a real `RECEIPT` to the main Stock Ledger immediately, even though the business doesn't own it yet; recording consumption ("ownership transfer") is a separate, manual reconciliation action that does not post a second stock movement. | Physically it's on the shelf and usable from day one; correlating exactly which physical units came from consignment vs. owned stock would require real lot-level ownership costing, deferred along with FIFO/FEFO valuation (Decision Log, 2026-09-08 "Consignment stock slice"). |
| BR-AG-13 | `Manage` (agreements, receiving stock) and `Convert` (recording consumption) on Consignment are separate permissions. | "Who can receive stock" and "who can authorize an ownership transfer for billing" are typically different roles — store staff vs. finance/procurement (Decision Log, 2026-09-08 "Consignment stock slice"). |
| BR-AG-14 | A Service Ticket has four separately-permissioned lifecycle stages (Manage/Assign/Resolve/Close), and Cancel is reachable only from `OPEN`/`IN_PROGRESS`. | A coordinator assigns, a technician resolves, and closing (with optional feedback) is often a different actor again; matches the module's standing "cancel only from an early state" shape (Decision Log, 2026-09-08 "Service Ticket slice"). |
| BR-AG-15 | Every entity in this sub-module reuses the platform's existing shared `audit_log` table under its own `entityType`, rather than a new parallel audit table. | Matches the ER diagram's own "no new AuditLog entity" finding (`ER_DIAGRAM_AND_MODULE_BOUNDARIES.md` §0/§8). |

## 4. Business Process / Workflow Narrative
A Budget is allocated against a Location for a date-range period; its consumed/remaining amounts are visible live as Purchase Orders are sent against that location, with an over-allocation flag surfaced but never enforced. Separately, an admin can author one or more Approval Workflows against Purchase Requisition or Purchase Order (optionally scoped by Location and, for Purchase Order, a minimum amount), each with an ordered chain of steps naming a required permission. When a document needs routing, an authorized user explicitly starts an Approval Instance against it; each stage's designated approver(s) act via Approve/Reject, or a holder of the dedicated bypass permission can override a stuck stage with a structured exception reason. A single rejection fails the whole instance immediately; clearing every stage completes it as Approved. Independently, physical items crossing the premises boundary — outward for repair/loan/event, or inward for a vendor's own tool/equipment — are tracked as a Gate Pass: requested, approved, then gate-verified by security (always a separate step), and, if returnable, later recorded as returned (going overdue automatically if past its expected date). A supplier can also place stock on a deployment's shelves under a Consignment Agreement before it's paid for: receiving it posts a real stock receipt immediately (it's usable right away), while a separate, manual "record consumption" action periodically reconciles how much of that vendor-owned stock has actually been used, for billing purposes, without touching the Stock Ledger a second time. Finally, any internal request for help — a complaint, a service/maintenance request — can be logged as a Service Ticket against a Location and a configurable Category, then assigned, resolved, and closed (with optional feedback), or cancelled early.

## 6. Assumptions & Constraints
- Assumes budget enforcement can remain advisory (visibility, not a hard block) for this phase — a future escalation to real spend-blocking is explicitly anticipated but not scoped here.
- Assumes an approval workflow being available is not the same as it being mandatory — a document can still be submitted/sent without ever passing through one, since the engine isn't wired as a submit-time gate.
- Assumes consignment billing/reconciliation happens outside the system (the business's own operational knowledge informs when to record consumption) — no invoice-generation or billing-cycle scheduler exists.
- Assumes no deployment currently needs quorum-style (partial N-of-M) approval — only "all must approve in parallel" or strict sequential chains are modeled.

## 7. Known Gaps / Deferred
See SRS.md §6: no submit-time approval gate wired into Purchase Requisition/Order services, budget enforcement is advisory only, no `PeriodType` flexibility on Budget (plain date range instead), no quorum threshold, no frontend permission-code picker, no dedicated Asset/PO pickers on Gate Pass, no automatic document generation or billing engine on Consignment consumption, and no outbound notifications at any stage of any workflow in this sub-module.
